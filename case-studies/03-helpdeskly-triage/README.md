# Helpdeskly: ticket triage without paying a frontier model to output a label

*LeanInference case study 3. Every number comes from a run on our 700-ticket, hand-verified test
set (`results/final_table.md`, regenerated by `python eval/report.py`); prices are in
`eval/prices.json`. Volume: 1,000,000 tickets/month.*

## What you have today

Every ticket goes to a frontier model (Claude Opus 5.5, $4 / $20 per million input / output tokens)
with a classification prompt, to get back three labels: intent (12 classes), urgency, sentiment. At
~200 tokens per ticket that is **about $1,120 a month** - for a model whose whole answer is ~16 tokens.

## What we tested (same 700 tickets for every approach)

| Approach | Intent macro-F1 (before → after) | High-urgency recall | Sentiment acc | p95 | Monthly $ | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline proxy: gpt-oss-120b, zero-shot | 0.969 | 0.943 | 0.779 | 2.76 s | $2,202 ¹ | API |
| A: MiniMax M3.1 Flash, few-shot prompt | 1.000 | 0.936 | 0.814 | 3.7 s | $563 ² | API |
| B1: MiniLM embeddings + logistic regression (no generation) | 0.608 → 0.972 | 0.936 | 0.837 | 0.02 s | $130 | 22M params |
| B2: Needle 3 embeddings + logistic regression (no generation) | 0.184 → 0.952 | 0.901 | 0.896 | 0.22 s | $130 | 63 MB |
| **C: Qwen3.5-0.8B fine-tuned (LoRA) to output the labels** | 0.551 → **1.000** | **0.943** | **1.000** | 0.98 s ³ | **$431** | 0.8B |
| D: Needle 3 fine-tuned (LoRA) as a classifier, `.cact` | 0.244 → 0.576 | 0.858 | 0.967 | 0.51 s | $130 | 63 MB |

¹ The baseline proxy completed all 700 tickets. Its measured token usage is priced at the
status quo model's list rates. ² A is priced at MiniMax M3 list
price (its closest paid listing) × its measured ~1,800-token few-shot prompt. ³ Single request on a
6 GB laptop GPU. Self-hosted monthly cost = always-on rented machines (L4 GPU $0.59/h; 4-vCPU c7i
$0.18/h) at measured latency.

**Honesty check:** 129 test tickets (18%) are near-duplicates of training tickets once order numbers
are masked. On the 571 tickets with no near-duplicate the results hold: C keeps intent 1.000,
sentiment 1.000 and high-urgency recall 0.937. The synthetic intents are simply easy - Candidate A
gets intent 1.000 with no training at all. Urgency and sentiment are where approaches differ.

## What we recommend

**C, the fine-tuned Qwen3.5-0.8B**:

- the only approach at or above the bar on both intent macro-F1 and high-urgency recall, and the best
  on everything else: urgency accuracy 0.989, sentiment accuracy 1.000;
- **$1,120 → $431 a month (-62%)**, on one rented GPU, inside your own cloud, no frontier fallback;
- 34 minutes of training on a laptop GPU; 0 malformed outputs in 700.

**If you want the cheapest option that is nearly as good: B1** - plain embeddings + logistic
regression, no text generation at all, $130/month on one small CPU machine (-88%), 20 ms per ticket.
Its intent macro-F1 of 0.972 clears the baseline bar, and its high-urgency recall
of 0.936 falls below the baseline bar of 0.943.

Needle 3 (B2 embeddings, D fine-tune) is the smallest, on-device option; its sentiment is strong
(0.967 for D) but its intent accuracy is not ready.

## What still goes wrong (details: `FAILURES.md`)

C's only errors: 8 of 700 tickets where the situation is urgent but the customer wrote "no rush" or
"when you get a minute" - the model followed the politeness. Every approach makes this mistake. A
two-line business rule ("account locked" and "change address on an open order" are always high)
removes 3 of the 8; more training examples of the pattern address the rest.

## How the numbers were produced

5,000 synthetic tickets (uneven classes, angry/short/long/multi-issue/non-English), train 4,000 / val
300 / test 700; the test set was hand-verified and never used for training or tuning (`data/`).
Classifier and fine-tuning scripts in `train/`, evaluation in `eval/`; every run, including the dead
ends, is in `results/experiment_log.jsonl`. Two measurement bugs we found on the way and fixed: the
Needle engine silently drops classifier answers whose labels are not words in the ticket (its own
`extract()` keeps them; intent 0.07 → 0.58 once read correctly), and the base Qwen model ignores the
output format (scored with a lenient parser, its format failures reported).
